Before You Try Anything Else

Incorrect handling of a failing drive permanently destroys data. Follow these strict guidelines.

DO:

  • Stop unnecessary use immediately.
  • Disconnect the affected drive safely from power.
  • Preserve the original device and its cables.
  • Seek a professional technical assessment.

DON'T:

  • Do not format the drive.
  • Do not initialize an unrecognized drive.
  • Do not repeatedly power-cycle a failing device.
  • Do not install unnecessary repair utilities.
  • Do not write new data to the affected storage.

FAQs

1. Can data be recovered if the drive is not detected?
Yes. A drive failing to be detected is often due to firmware corruption, controller failure, or read-head failure. We utilize specialized hardware to bypass the BIOS/OS and attempt to access the raw data directly.
2. Can deleted files be recovered?
Recovery feasibility depends on the type of failure, device condition, and the state of the underlying data. For HDDs, deleted files are often recoverable if not overwritten. For SSDs, features like TRIM can permanently clear deleted data quickly.
3. What should I do if my HDD is making clicking or unusual sounds?
Power it off immediately. Clicking usually indicates mechanical read/write head failure. Continued operation will cause the heads to scratch the magnetic platters, permanently destroying the data.
4. Is SSD data recovery different from HDD recovery?
Significantly. HDDs rely on mechanical platters, while SSDs use NAND flash memory and complex wear-leveling algorithms. SSD recovery often involves logically rebuilding the Flash Translation Layer (FTL) and bypassing damaged controllers.
5. Can data be recovered from an NVMe drive?
Yes, though it requires specific hardware to interface with PCIe protocols. Feasibility depends heavily on whether the NVMe controller has suffered catastrophic electrical failure or if the NAND memory itself is degraded.
6. Can a formatted drive be recovered?
Often yes, provided no new data was written to the drive after the format occurred. A "quick format" merely resets the file system directory, leaving the raw data intact until overwritten.
7. Should I stop using the drive immediately after data loss?
Absolutely. The operating system constantly writes background data (logs, caches). Any ongoing use of the drive drastically increases the chance that your lost data will be permanently overwritten.
8. What happens during the initial assessment?
We carefully inspect the device's physical condition, test basic electrical/logical responses without stressing the media, and determine a safe strategy. We then communicate the recovery feasibility before proceeding.
9. How long can a data recovery take?
Timelines vary heavily. A simple logical recovery might take 24–48 hours, whereas a complex mechanical rebuild requiring donor parts or intricate NAND reading can take a week or more. We provide estimates after diagnosis.
10. Is data recovery always possible?
No. Recovery feasibility depends on the type of failure, device condition, and the state of the underlying data. Severe platter scoring on an HDD or severe memory degradation on an SSD can render data permanently unrecoverable.
