Before You Try Anything Else

Incorrect handling of a failing drive permanently destroys data. Follow these strict guidelines.

DO:

  • Stop unnecessary use immediately.
  • Disconnect the affected drive safely from power.
  • Preserve the original device and its cables.
  • Seek a professional technical assessment.

DON'T:

  • Do not format or initialize an unrecognized drive.
  • Do not keep retrying or power-cycling a clicking device.
  • Do not run `chkdsk` or aggressive software repair tools.
  • Do not write any new data to the affected storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can data be recovered from a drive that is not detected?
Yes. A drive failing to be detected is often due to firmware corruption, controller failure, or read-head failure. We utilize specialized hardware to bypass the BIOS/OS and access the raw data directly.
Is SSD recovery different from HDD recovery?
Significantly. HDDs rely on mechanical platters, while SSDs use NAND flash memory and complex wear-leveling algorithms. SSD recovery often involves rebuilding the Flash Translation Layer (FTL) manually.
What should I do if my HDD is making clicking noises?
Power it off immediately. Clicking usually indicates mechanical read/write head failure. Continued operation will cause the heads to scratch the magnetic platters, permanently destroying the data.
Is recovery always possible?
No. Recovery feasibility strictly depends on the condition of the device. Severe platter damage on an HDD or severe encryption/TRIM execution on an SSD can render data permanently unrecoverable. This is why immediate professional diagnosis is critical.
